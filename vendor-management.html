Product context

A platform built on vendors.

SpringVerify is a background verification platform by Springworks. It helps HR, compliance, and ops teams automate candidate verification across education, employment, address, identity, and world checks.

The internal tool

The Super Admin Portal is the internal tool the ops team uses to onboard and manage vendors, configure check types, track escalations, and ensure SLAs are met.

This case study

This case study focuses on rebuilding its vendor management module.

What's the issue?

Five sheets, five headaches.

The Ops team managed vendors across Address, Education, Employment, Identity, and World checks. Each had its own Google Sheet. TAT and pricing lived in two more sheets that had to be re-uploaded any time anything changed.

Vendor assignment was manual — compare cities, prices, TAT for every candidate. Escalations lived in chat threads. Nothing was a system. Everything was a tab.

Fragmented data

Each check type had its own sheet, leading to inconsistent records and silent drift between tabs.

Duplicate uploads

TAT and pricing lived in separate files. A single update meant editing and re-uploading both.

Manual assignment

Comparing location, pricing, and TAT for every candidate slowed the entire verification pipeline.

Unstructured escalations

Vendor issues lived in chat threads — no unified view, no audit trail, no ownership.

No vendor visibility

There was no single view of vendor coverage, workload, or performance. Ops had to piece together information across different sources.

Check-specific ownership

Different verification checks could have different vendor contacts, but ownership and contact details weren't structured by check type.

Manual vendor onboarding

Adding a new vendor meant collecting details, contacts, TAT, and pricing separately before the vendor could be configured.

Hard to track TAT & pricing

Vendor TAT and pricing changed over time, but maintaining and finding the latest information depended on spreadsheets.

User needs

What Ops actually asked for.

I spoke to ten ops team members over two weeks. I watched them onboard vendors, update TAT, and chase escalations on Slack. Five clear needs emerged.

01

Centralized vendor management

A single, unified system covering all check types, eliminating multiple spreadsheets.

02

Faster data updates

A quicker way to update TAT and pricing without manually editing and re-uploading separate files.

03

Simplified escalation tracking

An integrated escalation flow to log, monitor, and resolve issues inside the platform.

04

Transparent vendor insights

Clear visibility into vendor performance, escalation history, and SLAs to drive decisions.

05

Reduced manual effort

Automation and prefilled data wherever possible — fewer keystrokes, fewer mistakes.

Before state

How data lived before.

All vendor details — city coverage, pricing, TAT — were maintained manually in Google Sheets. Each check type had its own sheet. Updates were independent. Drift was inevitable.

Drawing this out helped the team see the actual cost of "we'll just use a sheet for now."

Design decisions

Now look at some behind the scenes.

Three important decisions that shaped the product. Every design choice involved a tradeoff. Here are the three that mattered most.

Iteration 1

Check type workspace

+Closely maps to how verification work is organized
+Easy to understand vendor coverage by check
+Good for check-specific operations
–Doesn't provide enough overall vendor health
–Vendor-level management requires another step
Iteration 2

Vendor directory first

+Search and status filters make it easy to find and manage a specific vendor.
+Works well when the goal is to find, review, or update an individual vendor.
–Doesn't help Ops understand overall vendor health or identify where attention is needed.
–Ops needs to open individual vendors to understand their performance and workload.
Iteration 3

Performance dashboard view

+Gives Ops immediate operational context
+Performance issues are visible upfront
+Natural drill-down
+Supports monitoring before management
–More information on the first screen
–Requires stronger information hierarchy
Chose Option 3: Performance dashboard

We chose a performance-led overview because the primary need was to quickly understand what was happening across the vendor ecosystem before drilling into a specific check or vendor.

Rationale

Prioritize operational visibility over information density. Surface the signals that help Ops identify where attention is needed, while keeping detailed vendor information one click away.

The outcome

Spreadsheets out. System in.

How we measured

80% · Onboarding

Time-on-task study with 4 ops users onboarding a new vendor end-to-end. Pre-launch (Sheets) vs. post-launch on the new portal. Average dropped from ~25 mins to ~5.

40% · Escalation resolution

Pulled escalation entries from the new module's audit log over 30 days post-launch and compared average resolution against historical chat-thread tracking. Reported as directional given pre-period data quality.

82% · Data consistency

Sampled 130 vendor records and audited duplicate / inconsistent fields against the old multi-sheet baseline. Inconsistencies dropped from ~94 to ~17 — an ~82% reduction.

Note: Impact reflects early post-launch observations. Future iterations would benefit from structured funnel tracking and phased cohort comparisons for deeper validation.
Challenges & learnings

What I'd take into the next internal tool.

01

Sheets aren't a system

Spreadsheets gave the team flexibility, but fragmented workflows created version drift, duplicate work, and inconsistent data.

02

Internal users want speed, not novelty

Ops didn't need a flashy tool. They needed faster access to the right data, fewer manual steps, and confidence that the information was accurate.

03

Sheets aren't a system

When workflows depend on manual data entry, structuring the experience around check types, contacts, TAT and pricing reduces ambiguity before it becomes an operational issue.

04

Stakeholder rounds shape the brief

Early rounds with the CTO, CEO and Ops team helped clarify what needed to be solved — and prevented us from optimizing the wrong workflow.
